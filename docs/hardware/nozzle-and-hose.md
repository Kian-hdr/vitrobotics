# Bringing water to the aircraft

The nozzle adapter was the mechanical link between an existing aircraft and a cleaning tool. The initial Vitrobotics prototype used a modified DJI Inspire 1 Pro, identified by Kian as sponsored by Drone Masters Academy. Its cleaning system brought water from the ground through a hose to an aircraft-mounted nozzle. Making that arrangement useful required attention to the nozzle position, the attachment and the hose as a moving part of the system.

Kian identifies Factory Hammerbrooklyn in Hamburg as the building used for cleaning tests. The archived `Aeroshine_Flight_4_v2.0.0.mp4` shows the aircraft airborne with a hanging hose and spray directed at the façade. That visible result connects the mechanical work to its intended application; it does not establish which adapter export was installed.

## A family of adapters

The surviving files preserve several alternatives: `gd11_s1m1.3mf`, `gd21_v1.3mf` and `gd34.3mf`, alongside `top_plate.3mf`, `Hose_Holder_v2.3mf`, and the MK2 files `Bracket_1.3mf` and `Bracket_2.3mf`. The two inspected adapter packages identify their model as “Adapter”; the inspected MK2 package identifies “Bracket_1.”

Together these files provide a starting point for reconstructing the attachment system. Their names preserve candidate and revision distinctions, but do not establish a chronological sequence, a winning design or a flown configuration. In particular, the `gd` names alone cannot identify a generative study or its settings.

## From branching geometry to print preparation

Topology optimization and generative design were central to the project, according to Kian. `Screenshot 2024-11-20 at 10.00.15.png` provides a concrete view of the manufacturing preparation: a branching adapter form in the slicer, with support material around it.

The screenshot shows a Bambu Lab A1 with a 0.4 mm nozzle, Extrudr GreenTec pro CF filament and a 0.20 mm profile. Its estimates distinguish **82.14 g of model filament** from **106.93 g total filament**, with an estimated print time of **4 h 23 min**. These are recorded slicer estimates, not measurements of a finished part or confirmation that this print completed. They nevertheless preserve an important part of the design process: the geometry had reached print preparation, where supports and process settings became part of the practical build.

The inspected adapter exports contain meshes rather than a recovered optimization study. They do not supply the load cases, preserved interfaces, obstacle regions, material properties or selection criteria needed to explain why one generated alternative was chosen.

## Refinement through testing

The historical first-flight review records nozzle-position changes and redesign of the hose fixation after testing. It also describes hose resistance increasing as the aircraft climbed. These observations explain why the design story includes both the nozzle adapter and the hose holder: placing the spray tool was only one part of integrating the cleaning equipment with the aircraft.

The archive does not yet connect those field changes to a particular `gd` export or to `Hose_Holder_v2.3mf`. That connection remains to be reconstructed from original Fusion designs, installation photographs and dated build records. No tether load or structural margin is derived from the surviving account.

## What this case establishes

The project progressed through adapter alternatives, print preparation and physical cleaning tests, with recorded feedback on nozzle placement and hose management. The next useful addition is the correspondence between those stages: which design was manufactured, how it was attached, and what changed after its test.

The water hose does not establish electrical ground power. This case documents a cleaning attachment and its development, not a validated load rating or a complete manufacturing specification. The named source artifacts remain in the private archive; no CAD files are distributed with this page.
